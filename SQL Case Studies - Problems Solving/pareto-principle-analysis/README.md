# Pareto Principle (80/20 Rule) Analysis

## Problem

Analyze DVD rental activity to determine whether the **Pareto Principle** is observed.

The Pareto Principle states that:

> Approximately 80% of outcomes are generated by 20% of participants.

For this analysis:

- Identify the **top 20% of customers** based on the number of rentals made.
- Calculate the total number of rentals generated by these customers.
- Determine what percentage of all rentals they account for.

---

## Business Goal

Understanding customer concentration is valuable for:

- Customer segmentation
- Loyalty program design
- Revenue concentration analysis
- Marketing prioritization
- Identifying high-value customers

Organizations often want to know whether a small group of customers generates a disproportionate share of activity.

---

## Key Insight

The challenge was not calculating rental counts.

The challenge was correctly interpreting:

> Top 20% of customers

The requirement specifies:

```text
Take exactly 20% of customers
```

If the customer count is not divisible by 5, the result must be rounded up.

Example:

```text
599 customers

599 × 20% = 119.8

Top customers to include = 120
```

This means the solution cannot rely solely on percentile functions that may include or exclude additional rows because of ties.

Instead, customers must be explicitly ranked and the top N customers selected.

## Approach

### Step 1: Count Rentals Per Customer

Calculate:

```text
Customer
→
Total Rentals
```

Example:

| Customer | Rentals |
|-----------|---------:|
| A | 52 |
| B | 47 |
| C | 45 |

---

### Step 2: Rank Customers

Sort customers by rental count in descending order.

Use:

```sql
ROW_NUMBER()
```

to assign a unique ranking.

Example:

| Rank | Customer | Rentals |
|--------:|----------|---------:|
| 1 | A | 52 |
| 2 | B | 47 |
| 3 | C | 45 |

---

### Step 3: Determine Top 20%

Calculate:

```text
CEIL(total_customers × 0.20)
```

This guarantees the correct number of customers are included.

---

### Step 4: Sum Their Rentals

Aggregate rental counts for only the selected customers.

---

### Step 5: Calculate Contribution Percentage

Compute:

```text
Top 20% Rentals
----------------
Total Rentals
```

and convert to a percentage.

## Pattern Learned

### Top N Percent Analysis

A common analytical requirement is:

```text
Top X% of entities
```

Examples:

- Top 10% of customers by spending
- Top 5% of products by revenue
- Top 20% of employees by performance
- Top 1% of users by activity

A reliable approach is:

1. Rank entities.
2. Calculate the required count.
3. Select the top N rows.
4. Aggregate results.

---

### Business Rule vs SQL Function

An important lesson from this challenge:

The business requirement was:

```text
Exactly 20% of customers
```

not:

```text
All customers within the top 20th percentile
```

This distinction matters because percentile-based functions may handle ties differently than the business expects.

## Challenges Encountered

The first instinct was to use:

```sql
CUME_DIST()
```

because the requirement involved a percentage of customers.

However, the problem specifically required selecting an exact number of customers:

```text
CEIL(total_customers × 0.20)
```

This led to switching from percentile logic to explicit ranking using:

```sql
ROW_NUMBER()
```

which better matched the business requirement.

This was a useful reminder that understanding the requirement is often more important than choosing the most advanced SQL function.
